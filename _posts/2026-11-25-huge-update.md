#### Power Supply\...
First, the one project that we had and didn't drop <span class="dark-text">(kinda)</span> was a power-supply! Specifically, a 12V dual power-supply for modular synthesis purposes.

We actually completed a first revision of the project, which seemed to work as intended, though there were some complications.

I probably won't go too much into details of the project itself in this post, as we'll likely make a dedicated post once it's finished, but\...

We are on revision **two**, with boards and components on hand and everything. Just need to solder it\... <span class="dark-text">Which we already had but it didn't work out the first time.</span>

The primary complication and source of procrastination at this point is the fact it is almost entirely an <span class="hint-text" title="'surface-mount technology' ; 'surface-mount device' ; 'annoyingly tiny little components often unsolderable with a soldering iron'">**SMD**</span> board.

The process of assembling a board takes roughly six hours. The process is as follows;
- **Apply solder paste to the board via a stencil.** Do this with <span style="text-decoration: underline;">micrometer</span> precision! Do not smear anything! Or you're toast! Hehe!
- **Place each *(tiny)* component one-by-one on the board.** Do not be off with your alignment, even by a hundred microns! Or you're toast! Hehe!
- **Solder the board on a hotplate.** Be sure the heat distribution is even and a sufficient reflow curve is followed! Or you're toast! Hehe!
- **Solder the through-hole components.** This one isn't that bad. Also, fix any errors on the reflow soldered joints such as bridges - flux is surprisingly good at this!
- **Test the board.** Cry if it doesn't work. Or you're toast! Hehe!

|

Needless to say it is a very slow, precise and painful process. But enough of that\...
